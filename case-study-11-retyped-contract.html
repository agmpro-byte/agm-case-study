Now the sheet fills itself from the contract, and a mismatch stops the job before it reaches the schedule.

The client

Every job is copied from one paper to the next

At Heavenly Greens, each job travels from the signed contract to the commission sheet to the installer's packet. Every step is a chance for the job to change without anyone meaning it to.

The moment
How did the wrong turf get to the job?

In early November, a job went out with the wrong turf. The cause was simple: the sales rep had typed the turf onto the commission sheet by hand, and the sheet, not the contract, is what the rest of the process read.

The investigation

We checked every filed job

The same afternoon, every filed commission sheet was compared with its signed contract.

Filed jobsMatched the contractDidn't matchCouldn't be checked
776764

One example: the contract said 1,438 square feet. The sheet said 1,363. Nobody lied and nobody was careless on purpose. Someone retyped a number.

What we built

Stop the retyping, and stop the job if it happens anyway

November 4, 1:09pm

The sheet fills itself

At signing, the commission sheet is pre-filled from the signed contract, with a check line: contract says this, the sheet says that.

November 4, 2:07pm

A mismatch emails the rep and the office

November 6

Why this matters

Every retype is a place for it to go wrong

For the owner

A wrong-turf job costs the material, the crew's day, the trip back and the customer's patience.

For the sales rep

Nobody wants to be the person who typed the wrong number. Don't make them type it.

For the installer

The crew should install what the customer signed for, not what someone copied.

For the office

A mismatch caught before scheduling is a phone call. Caught at the job, it's a redo.

Try this in your business this week

Find one place your team retypes something

  1. Pick one hand-off: contract to order, quote to invoice, job to commission sheet.
  2. Compare the last 50 side by side.
  3. If any don't match, make the second document fill itself from the first.
