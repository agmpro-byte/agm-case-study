The client

A call center that runs on one list

Heavenly Greens' call center books every appointment the company runs. Its day runs off a call-back board: every missed call, ranked, so nobody who rang goes unanswered.

When a call isn't picked up, an automatic text goes out straight away: sorry we missed you, we'll call you back. It's the kind of automation every contractor is told to set up.

The moment
“Why isn't she on the board?”

Thursday, August 20, between 4:43 and 5:03pm: one customer called six times. Each missed call got our auto-text. She never appeared on the call-back board.

The next morning Troy saw the calls on her customer record and asked the question above. Nobody on the call center could have known: the board looked clean.

The investigation

The automation hid the thing it was answering

The board was built to find conversations whose last message was a missed call. In July that rule had been checked against 6,687 call conversations, and it held.

Then the auto-text started replying. Every reply made our own text the newest message, so to the board, the conversation looked dealt with. The customer who tried hardest to reach us was the one it hid.

Why this matters

Any automation that replies for you can hide what it replied to

For the owner

The auto-text is on the checklist of every marketing course. Nobody checks what it does to the list your call center works from.

For the call center

A list is only as good as the rule that builds it, and nobody on the floor can see the rule.

For the customer

Six calls in twenty minutes is someone ready to buy, or someone with a problem. Either way, they are the person you most need to call back.

For the money

If the call came from a Google Local Services ad, you may have paid for it: Google charges for some missed calls during your listed hours.

Try this in your business this week

Check your call-back list against your missed-call log

  1. Pull yesterday's missed calls straight from your phone system.
  2. Put them next to your call-back list.
  3. For anyone missing, look at the last message in their conversation. If it's your own automation, you've found the leak.
