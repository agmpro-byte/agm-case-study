The client

A good platform, doing its job

A pest-control operator in California's Central Valley: about 2,500 active customers, with routing, scheduling and billing all running on one of the industry's leading practice-management platforms. The platform is genuinely good at that job, and nothing here changes it.

AGM Pro Tools runs alongside it: a dashboard on a sync three times a day that turns the raw records into ranked lists of who to collect from, who is about to cancel, and where the next sale is.

The question
“Can you show me how these numbers compare to last year?”

Looking at recurring revenue, active customers, cancellation rate and autopay coverage, the owner asked the most natural question in business. Every owner assumes the answer lives in their software somewhere.

It doesn't. Finding out exactly why became the most important finding of the engagement.

The discovery

Operational software stores today. It does not store history.

The platform's job is to answer “what needs to happen today?”, and it answers brilliantly. That means “what was my recurring revenue last June?” isn't hidden or hard to reach. It doesn't exist. No report, export or connection can produce a number the system never wrote down.

What the platform keeps

  • Today's customers and plans
  • Today's balances
  • Today's routes and schedule
  • Every invoice ever sent

What it overwrites

  • Autopay enrollment: last month's is gone
  • Balances and aging: yesterday's no longer exists
  • When a customer left: over 4,000 of about 6,900 inactive records had no date
Invoices remember revenue. Almost nothing remembers the state of the business on a given day.
The reconstruction

90 days back, matched to the penny

One partial exception: recent cancellation records still held their dates, so the last 90 days could be rebuilt from raw account and subscription records.

We checked the method the honest way: we rebuilt today's numbers from the raw records alone and compared them with the live figures. They matched to the penny. The same day the owner asked, every number on the scoreboard carried a plus-or-minus against 30 days earlier.

But 90 days was the ceiling. Reconstruction could buy a month of hindsight. It could never buy a year.

What we built

A record that writes itself, every day

Once a day, the sync now banks a permanent snapshot of the business: nine numbers, written down and never overwritten.

Why it compounds

This history cannot be backfilled

If the record starts in July, it starts in July. There is no buying the missing months back.

Every day it runs, it gets more valuable: real seasonal curves, early warning when autopay coverage starts to slip, and the difference between “this summer feels better” and “this summer is 6% better.” For an owner who ever sells, it is the operating history a buyer wishes every business had.

Try this in your business this week

Find the numbers your software overwrites

  1. Pick five numbers you'd want to compare year over year: active customers, autopay share, recurring revenue.
  2. Try to pull each one for the same month last year.
  3. Any you can't pull is being erased every day. Start writing them down today, even in a spreadsheet.
