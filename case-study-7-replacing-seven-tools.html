Replacing seven tools: over $100,000 a year

For eleven years, Heavenly Greens ran on the same Salesforce setup, with more tools added around it. Here is how one system is replacing seven of them.

Who’s who: Troy Scott owns AGM Pro Tools and is a partner in Heavenly Greens. This is our own company’s story, told with its own numbers.

Eleven years of adding tools

Heavenly Greens installs artificial turf. Its Salesforce setup was first built in July 2015. Over the years, more programs were added around it.

By 2025, that software was costing the company over $100,000 a year. And fitting it to anything new meant paying for more custom work.

Two decisions in 2025

First: stop patching

Build one system around how Heavenly Greens actually works, from the first phone call to the final payment, instead of adding to the old setup.

Second: if it worked for us, offer it to other contractors

That second decision is AGM Pro Tools.

The aim was never just to get off Salesforce. It was to make the company’s work, its information, its software costs and its decisions fit together.

Seven tools, one system

The new system is replacing seven separate tools, Salesforce among them. Together, those seven cost Heavenly Greens over $100,000 a year.

Six of the seven have already been replaced. Salesforce is on its way out: new sales stopped going through it on October 5, 2026. Jobs that were already in it finish there, and its history stays readable.

What one system shows that seven never did

When sales moved off Salesforce in October, the new system started catching things the old setup never showed us:

  • Missed calls that hid behind our own “sorry we missed you” auto-text now land on one call-back list.
  • Every sales rep starts the day with a short list of customers waiting on an answer.
  • Dashboard numbers are checked against the appointments behind them. (Why that matters)
  • Every finished job stays on one list until it’s paid in full. The first time we ran it, it found about $80,000 owed to us. (That story)
  • The rules for how money moves, like prices, commissions and approvals, are written down, so the system follows them instead of someone’s memory.

Try this on your own business

  1. List every piece of software you pay for.
  2. Next to each one, write what it does and what it costs in a year.
  3. Ask which of those jobs one system, built around how your business actually works, could do instead.

Source: the seven tools and the over-$100,000 yearly figure are Troy Scott’s own first-hand account of Heavenly Greens’ software costs, given in November 2026. The July 2015 start date and the October 5, 2026 date come from Heavenly Greens’ own records.
