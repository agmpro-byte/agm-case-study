The client

A turf and landscaping company that outgrew its software

Heavenly Greens is an artificial turf and landscaping company that has worked across Northern California since 2003, with nearly $400 million in lifetime sales. It sells through two channels: direct to homeowners, through its own marketing on TV, in print, online, at events and across the rest of the standard home-improvement mix; and a partnership with Costco, serving 14 Costco stores in Northern California.

Five departments carry every job, and each one picks up where the one before it left off:

  • The call center turns incoming leads, from Heavenly Greens' own marketing and from Costco, into booked appointments.
  • Sales reps sit at the kitchen table, measure the yard, price the job and write the contract, starting from what the call center typed.
  • Install crews build what the contract says: the turf, the square footage, the details of the site.
  • The service team handles what comes after the install, working from the record of what went in the ground.
  • Accounting collects every dollar, from the deposit to the final payment, on the jobs everyone before it recorded.

So every department depends on what the others typed into the software. When one entry is late or wrong, every department after it inherits the mistake.

The problem
Why does every new requirement cost us another custom build?

Heavenly Greens didn't have a CRM at all until around 2005, and back then CRM software was hard for a contractor to manage. It tried a custom-built system first, then settled on MarketSharp, and that stayed in place for nearly a decade. The second setup was started in 2014 and went live in July 2015, and it is the one being replaced today. Salesforce held the contracts, the project database, the appointments and the sales. Over the next eleven years, every gap it left got a tool of its own:

  • HubSpot for CRM and marketing automation
  • Invoca for call tracking
  • JivoChat for website chat
  • Broadly for review requests
  • Boast for customer testimonials
  • DocuSign for e-signatures

Every one of them was an annual subscription. By 2025 they added up to over $100,000 a year, and that was never a one-time cost: it came due again every year, for as long as the tools stayed. Each tool held its own piece of the customer's data from the buyer's journey, and the leaks lived in the gaps between them. When the Costco partnership needed new contract and reporting steps, fitting them into the old setup meant paying for substantial custom work.

Into 2027

Proving it beyond our own company

The same system now runs on top of software other companies already pay for, at every size:

  • An international turf manufacturer. One multi-tenant build for its dealer centers in several markets, integrated directly with Odoo, the manufacturer's ERP. Web leads land in one hub and go by ZIP code to the right center, each center works its own leads in its own private account, and AGM Pro Tools reads the orders, inventory and invoices straight out of Odoo.
  • A pest-control operator with about 2,500 customers. The owner had never seen the business laid out like this: nine numbers on one dashboard, banked every day, with the last 90 days rebuilt from raw records and matched to the penny. Year over year now arrives on the first anniversary instead of never.
  • An electrical contractor on a high-end field service subscription. Like most owners we work with, they never set up everything their software can do. Setup is overwhelming, so whole features sit unused. AGM Pro Tools sits on top of the system they already pay for and puts those features to work, so the owner gets what they bought it for in the first place: clarity, and operational intelligence.

In these three examples, none of them had to replace their CRM or field service software. AGM Pro Tools works on top of what they already run, and its job is to move two numbers: Autonomous Operating Yield, so more of the work finishes without anyone stepping in, and Operational Quotient, so every desk gets its work done faster.

Try this in your business this week

Have you added up your software bills?

  1. List every software subscription you pay for, with what it costs in a year and when it renews.
  2. Add up the yearly total. It is not a one-time cost: the same bill comes due next year, and the year after.
  3. Next to each one, write the one job it does.
  4. Circle every job that needs two tools to agree: a call that should become an appointment, a finished job that should become a final payment. That is where your leaks are.
  5. Ask whether one system built around how you work could do those jobs instead.
