The client

A turf and landscaping company that outgrew its software

Heavenly Greens is an artificial turf and landscaping company that has worked across Northern California since 2001, with about $400 million in sales so far. It sells to homeowners directly and through a national retail partner program. A call center books the appointments. Sales reps sit at kitchen tables. Crews install, a service team handles what comes after, and accounting chases every dollar in between.

That is five departments, and every one of them depends on what the others typed into the software.

The problem
Why does every new requirement cost us another custom build?

Heavenly Greens got its first CRM around 2006. The second setup was started in 2014 and went live in July 2015, and it is the one being replaced today. Salesforce held the contracts, the project database, the appointments and the sales. Over the next eleven years, every gap it left got a tool of its own:

  • HubSpot for CRM and marketing automation
  • Invoca for call tracking
  • JivoChat for website chat
  • Broadly for review requests
  • Boast for customer testimonials
  • DocuSign for e-signatures

By 2025, the software around the business cost about $100,000 a year. Each tool held its own copy of the customer, and the leaks lived in the gaps between them. When the retail partner program needed new contract and reporting steps, fitting them into the old setup meant paying for substantial custom work.

Try this in your business this week

Price your own software tax

  1. List every piece of software you pay for, with what it costs in a year.
  2. Next to each one, write the one job it does.
  3. Circle every job that needs two tools to agree: a call that should become an appointment, a finished job that should become a final payment. That is where your leaks are.
  4. Ask whether one system built around how you work could do those jobs instead.
