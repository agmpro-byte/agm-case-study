The client

Finishing the job is the easy part

Heavenly Greens finishes jobs every week: installs, repairs, service calls. Finishing the work is the satisfying part. Getting paid in full is the part that slips.

The problem
Where is the money for the work we've already done?

On August 12, Troy put a number on it: about $80,000 of finished work with no closed loop. Not lost, not disputed. Just nobody asking for it.

The service board showed what to schedule and what to finish. Nothing on it said collect.

The investigation

Three systems, and nobody owned the last question

The crew marks the job finished in the schedule. The invoice lives in the books. The payment arrives through the processor. Each system was right about its own piece. None of them owned the only question that matters: is this job paid in full?

Finished in the scheduleInvoiced in the booksPaidWho was asking?

The first count, on November 4: 41 finished jobs not paid in full. The oldest had waited 581 days. That is a different count from the $80,000 estimate, made a different way, and both pointed at the same gap.

What we built

One button for every kind of money waiting

The top of the service board now has one button per kind of work, each with a count and how long the oldest has waited. Money has three:

  • Invoices to send: the work is done and the customer hasn't been invoiced.
  • Collect payment: the invoice is out and the money isn't in, with a tracker showing whether the customer opened it. After 7 days the row turns red.
  • Call about the unpaid invoice: at that point the call center gets a line on its own list to call the customer.

Why this matters

The most expensive leak is money you've already earned

For the owner

You paid for the materials, the crew and the trip. Every unpaid finished job is a loan you never agreed to make.

For accounting

The books are right about what was invoiced. They can't tell you what was never invoiced at all.

For the crew

Finished means finished in the field. Nobody in the field owns whether it got paid.

For cash flow

The older a balance gets, the harder it is to collect. 581 days is a very old balance.

Try this in your business this week

Build the list this week, in any CRM or spreadsheet

  1. List every job you finished in the last two years.
  2. Next to each: invoiced? paid in full?
  3. Sort by days since it was finished.
  4. Anything unpaid after 30 days gets a phone call this week.
