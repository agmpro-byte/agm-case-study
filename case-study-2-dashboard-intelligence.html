The client

A sales program run across three systems

Heavenly Greens sells turf through a national retail partner program. The partner's call center sends the leads, Heavenly Greens books the appointments, and sales reps sit with the homeowners.

The lead arrived in one system. The appointment lived in another. The sale was recorded in a third. The dashboard was supposed to stitch them together.

The problem
“How many appointments did we run this month?”

A question any contractor should answer in seconds. At Heavenly Greens it took three systems, and the answer was still wrong.

24+ hrs

The dashboard hadn't refreshed in over a day. It only updated when someone remembered to run it by hand.

Missing sales

Two sales were invisible because the pipeline's stage names didn't match what the dashboard looked for.

Empty

No product breakdown

Which turf sold, how much, and for how much: blank, because the dashboard read the wrong field.

1 vs 8

Phantom appointments

It counted appointments by the day the lead arrived, not the day the appointment happened. Set this week showed 1. The real number was 8.

The investigation

Three systems, zero agreement

We traced every number on the dashboard back to the record behind it. Each system had its own name for the same stage and its own date for the same appointment.

Three months later

Then the close rate was wrong too

In June, the yearly close rate read 49.2%. The real figure was 62.5%: deals that were still open were being counted as lost. Loose stage matching had also overstated revenue by about $161,000.

This time the fix was a rule, not a patch: every number gets one definition, used everywhere. Close rate is won divided by won plus lost. A deal that is still open is neither.

Close rate, as shown
49.2%

Open deals counted as lost.

Close rate, calculated right
62.5%

Won ÷ (won + lost). Open deals wait.

The same deals, two formulas. A sales team judged on the left-hand number looks 13 points worse than it is.

Why this matters

Every contractor running more than one system has this problem

For the owner

If the dashboard says 3 appointments ran when the real number is 30, every decision about staffing, marketing and forecasting is built on the wrong number.

For the sales team

The top sales rep ran 30 appointments. The dashboard said 3. That isn't a data bug; it's a morale problem. Your best performer's work was invisible.

For the partner conversation

Sit across from a partner with numbers that are wrong and you lose credibility you don't get back.

For the business

Three systems that don't agree means three sources of truth, which means none.

Try this in your business this week

Audit one number on your dashboard

  1. Pick the number you act on most: close rate, appointments run, revenue this month.
  2. Ask how it's calculated. Which date does it count by? Which stages count as won, and as lost?
  3. Count one week by hand from the records behind it.
  4. If the two don't match, every decision built on that number is off by the same amount.
