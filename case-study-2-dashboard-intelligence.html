How it looked then

In March 2026, Heavenly Greens tracked its sales in Salesforce, while its leads and pipeline stages lived in a second system. The sales dashboard had to stitch the two together, and the two didn’t agree on basic facts.

That changed on October 5, 2026, when new sales stopped going through Salesforce. The lesson from that evening didn’t.

Why the numbers were wrong

Nothing was broken in an obvious way. The dashboard counted appointments by the date the lead came in, not the date of the appointment. One sales stage was named slightly differently in the two systems, so two sales never made it onto the screen. And nothing refreshed the data on its own; it was only as fresh as the last time someone ran it by hand.

Why it mattered

For the owner

If the dashboard says 3 appointments ran this month and the real number is 30, every call about staffing, marketing and forecasting is made on the wrong picture.

For the sales team

The top sales rep ran 30 appointments that month. The dashboard said 3. The best performer’s work was invisible.

What we changed that night

  • Appointments were counted by the date of the appointment, taken from the sales record.
  • Every pipeline stage was mapped by its exact name, so no sale could fall through a naming gap.
  • The dashboard refreshed itself every five minutes instead of waiting for someone to run it.
  • The numbers matched the top sales rep’s actual calendar.

The same month, a different symptom

Earlier that March, the same two systems had disagreed somewhere worse: on documents customers signed. An old automation linking them was writing job details into customers’ names, so a signed form could show the job’s description stuck to the end of the customer’s name. We traced it, replaced the automation, and corrected 176 contact records.

What you can take from it, with or without our software

Before you act on any number on a dashboard, ask how it’s calculated. Then check one by hand: count last week’s appointments on the calendar and compare. If they don’t match, the screen is guessing.
